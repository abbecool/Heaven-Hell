# Demon Drain and Possession Ability: Reviewed Notes

## Canon Alignment

The possession system supports the current story rather than replacing it with a generic class switcher.

- The player is a weak demon or spirit.
- It first drains a chicken on the Six Day Battlefield.
- It must then get past the unknowing battlefield guard.
- Its first major host is chosen from a dwarf, elf, wizard, or knight on the merchant road.
- Every host is an existing person, so possession carries social as well as mechanical consequences.

The player is Gudloth returned, but the mechanic should not assume that any modern faction recognizes the weak demon or spirit as an ancient returning power.

## Recommended Model

Keep drain and possession as two outcomes of one clearly telegraphed interaction. This preserves the idea of a single supernatural power while giving the player an intentional choice.

- **Tap Q — Drain:** harvest a weaker target for recovery.
- **Hold Q — Possess:** attempt to take control of an eligible target.
- If technical or combat clarity requires it, use two separate actions instead; the important distinction is that players understand the consequence before committing.

## Drain

- Best against weak mobs or targets that are not valuable as hosts.
- Starts a ritual with visible energy transfer and a clear interruption state.
- Can require temporary HP expenditure, leaving the demon at risk.
- Kills the target on completion and restores HP, optionally with a bonus multiplier.
- Should be fast and readable enough to use in the prologue on the chicken.

## Possession

- Transfers control to a host and grants that body's abilities, faction access, and social identity.
- The demon's core inventory and a portion of its progression persist between hosts.
- The host inventory can remain body-specific; item-transfer limits and UI need definition.
- Individual hosts can have separate level caps or attributes, encouraging later switches.
- Detection, suspicious behavior, and host obligations can create story consequences.

## Interaction States

1. Target an eligible mob.
2. Begin the TAKE OVER interaction and show a visual, audio, and UI cue.
3. Paralyze the target while making the demon vulnerable to interruption.
4. Resolve as drain or possession according to the chosen input and eligibility.
5. For possession, update faction targeting, control, inventory access, and current-host UI immediately.
6. For drain, kill the target and apply the health reward.
7. On interruption or failure, communicate the cost and recovery state clearly.

A longer two-stage ritual—drain the target to incapacity, then press Q again to possess—remains a viable alternative. It gives players a chance to change their mind, but needs clear UI and careful pacing.

## Questions Still Requiring Design Decisions

1. What exact stat determines whether a target is eligible: CPossessable, HP, demon power, target condition, or several factors?
2. Is possession temporary, permanent until death, or limited by a resource?
3. Does interruption cost HP, progress, a charge, or only time?
4. Can the player return to demon form safely and revisit former hosts?
5. Which targets are immune, protected by rituals, or too important to possess freely?
6. How do memories, relationships, disguises, and faction detection expose the player?
7. What persists between hosts besides the demon inventory?
8. How are friendly AI, enemies, and active quests reassigned when the host changes?
9. How will the UI distinguish the demon inventory from the current host inventory?
10. Are altars required for switching, optional stabilization points, or unrelated to possession?
