# Revised World History and Story Structure

This document summarizes the current direction for the world's history and early story progression. It builds heavily on the original world-history timeline while modifying it to fit the newer faction and possession-based story.

Many details surrounding Gudloth, the demon, and the Six Day Battle remain intentionally unresolved.

---

## 1. Historical Timeline

### Year 0 — The Visit

The existing calendar begins with an ancient event known as **The Visit**.

What The Visit actually was remains unknown. It may have been a divine event, magical event, arrival of some entity, or simply the traditional beginning of recorded history.

The Visit occurred when both known cyclical forces reached their peaks:

- **Dark or ancient magic** peaks every 903 years. It is the force Gudloth primarily uses.
- **Nature magic** peaks every 40 years and serves as a counterbalancing force.

Because 903 and 40 share no factors, the two forces peak together only once every 36,120 years. Their shared peak at Year 0 made the Visit an exceptionally rare magical convergence.

---

## 2. The Forge Kingdom

For centuries, the **Forge Kingdom** was the region's largest city and its dominant political and economic center. Smaller cities and settlements depended on its trade, protection, industry, and broader economic support.

The Forge Kingdom was not exclusively human. Its population and armies included several peoples:

- Humans
- Dwarves
- Elves
- Organized mages

Dwarven warriors and craftsmen, elven archers, human soldiers, and powerful mages lived and worked within the same broader civilization. This multicultural cooperation created substantial economic growth for all peoples connected to the kingdom.

Elves still maintained settlements in the forests, but those communities depended on economic support from the Forge Kingdom and on the elves who lived and worked in its city. Dwarves served as soldiers, blacksmiths, and other craftsmen in the kingdom; other dwarf communities mined the mountains and supplied ore for weapons, tools, and city industry. Wizards lived in the kingdom in smaller numbers, often as scholars, scientists, librarians, and students of the natural world.

Some wizards studied mysterious ancient magic that was poorly understood even then. This tradition of research became significant to Gudloth's story.

This ancient coexistence contrasts strongly with the divided factions of the modern world.

---

## 3. Rise of Gudloth

The old version of the lore surrounding Gudloth remains the current placeholder for his origin.

Gudloth was an exceptionally powerful wizard originating from the Enchanted Lands. He rejected the traditions of his homeland and eventually entered the Old Woods.

He spent decades studying, meditating, and developing his magical abilities, including the poorly understood ancient magic investigated by earlier scholars. His power eventually became so great that he was effectively a demigod.

Gudloth left the woods and began moving through the surrounding lands. Attempts to stop him failed, and followers gradually gathered around him. The most powerful became his apprentices.

Eventually Gudloth came into direct conflict with the Forge Kingdom.

Gudloth is the player: the weak demon or spirit-like entity that awakens at the start of the game is Gudloth returned in a diminished form. This is a late-story twist, not information given to the player at the beginning. His precise motives and the nature of his defeat in the Six Day Battle remain unresolved. The magical condition of his return is established: peak nature/life force combines with strong dark ancient magic in Year 1720.

---

## 4. Year 903 — The Six Day Battle

The major historical turning point occurred in **903 After the Visit**.

The Forge Kingdom confronted Gudloth and his followers on the rock plains.

The resulting conflict became known as the **Six Day Battle**.

The Forge Kingdom committed enormous military forces to the battle. Despite its strength, the kingdom was devastated.

The battle occurred at a peak of dark magic. Nature magic was almost at its lowest point: 903 years is 23 years into its 40-year cycle, just beyond its trough at roughly 20 years. This imbalance helped make Gudloth's power exceptionally dangerous.

The battle effectively destroyed the Forge Kingdom.

Gudloth was also defeated, destroyed, sealed, or otherwise removed from the world.

Exactly what happened to Gudloth remains unknown and should remain an important story mystery.

### Immediate consequences

The Six Day Battle caused enormous casualties and the collapse of regional civilization.

In particular:

- The Forge Kingdom ceased to exist as a major political power.
- The dwarven population of this region was completely wiped out.
- Elven survivors retreated away from the destroyed kingdom.
- Surviving mages scattered or retreated toward the wizard regions, including the Enchanted Lands.
- Human survivors dispersed into smaller settlements.
- Large parts of the old civilization were abandoned.
- The battlefield itself became a ruin.

Gudloth disappeared from history.

The surviving wizards continued studying the mysterious ancient magic in the centuries after the kingdom's decay. Their research is incomplete and does not mean they understand Gudloth or the Six Day Battle completely.

---

## 5. The Lost Period

The modern game begins in **Year 1720 After the Visit**, exactly 817 years after the Six Day Battle.

At that time, nature magic is at a full peak because 1,720 is divisible by 40. Dark magic is strong but not at its next peak, which will occur in Year 1806. The combined condition of peak life force and strong dark ancient magic is what allows Gudloth to return to life in a weakened form.

During this time:

- The Forge Kingdom became ancient history.
- Its ruins were abandoned, buried, reused, or forgotten.
- Its northern city lay abandoned for a long time before later generations reinhabited its remains and began calling it the Old City.
- Knowledge of Gudloth gradually disappeared.
- Stories about dwarves became mythology.
- Civilization recovered and reorganized into new political structures.
- The modern factions developed independently of the old Forge Kingdom.

The Six Day Battle remains part of history and folklore, but its actual events are poorly understood.

A modern person might know that an ancient kingdom supposedly fell after a catastrophic battle without knowing anything reliable about Gudloth or what actually happened.

---

## 6. Founding of La Poise

**La Poise was not founded by Gudloth.**

This modifies the original lore.

La Poise developed independently sometime after the destruction of the Forge Kingdom.

Human survivors and later generations established settlements around the Lace River and its fertile surroundings. Over generations, these settlements grew into the modern city.

La Poise therefore belongs to a **successor civilization**, rather than Gudloth's old empire.

Its inhabitants have no allegiance to Gudloth.

The modern knights have no connection to Gudloth and do not expect his return.

They are ordinary inhabitants of the modern world dealing with modern political problems.

---

## 7. Modern La Poise and the Knight Faction

La Poise has grown considerably.

Population growth has increased the city's need for:

- Food
- Farmland
- Resources
- Territory

This has pushed the city outward into surrounding lands.

In particular, expansion into forests and fertile territory has created conflict with the elves.

The knights protect the city, settlements, farmers, roads, and territorial expansion.

From their perspective, this expansion is necessary to support the growing population.

The knight faction is therefore **not an evil faction and not connected to the demon**.

The knight guarding the ancient battlefield at the beginning of the game has no idea what the player is.

---

## 8. The Elves

Elves survived the destruction of the Forge Kingdom and continued living elsewhere in the region.

Unlike humans, their culture may have preserved stronger oral traditions concerning the ancient world.

However, these traditions have changed over centuries and should not necessarily be historically accurate.

The elves currently oppose the expansion of La Poise into forests and other lands they consider theirs.

This creates the major modern conflict between the knight and elf factions.

Neither side should be completely right.

The humans require additional resources and farmland.

The elves see ancient forests and territory being destroyed by an expanding city.

---

## 9. The Return of the Dwarves

The dwarves who lived within the ancient Forge Kingdom were completely wiped out during the Six Day Battle.

For centuries afterward, **no dwarves lived in this part of the world**.

However, dwarven civilizations continued to exist elsewhere.

Recently, a new dwarven expedition arrived from another part of the world, probably from the west and potentially by sea.

These dwarves established the modern dwarf settlement near the mountains.

They are therefore not simply descendants who continuously occupied the old Forge Kingdom territory. They are newcomers rediscovering a land where other dwarves lived centuries earlier.

### Dwarves as mythology

Because no dwarves have been seen locally for centuries, most inhabitants have never seen one.

For many people, dwarves were believed to be:

- Extinct
- Exaggerated historical accounts
- Creatures from folklore
- Completely fictional

The recent arrival of actual dwarves is therefore a significant event.

People may react to dwarves with:

- Fear
- Curiosity
- Fascination
- Suspicion
- Disbelief

The first dwarven traders and explorers may even become minor celebrities.

A human encountering a dwarf might genuinely say:

> "I thought your kind were stories."

### Dwarven historical mystery

The new dwarves can gradually discover evidence that another dwarven population once lived here.

They may encounter:

- Ancient dwarven mines
- Dwarven weapons
- Graves
- Forge Kingdom ruins
- Old inscriptions
- Abandoned settlements

Their language and culture may have diverged enough that ancient inscriptions are recognizable but difficult to understand.

This gives the dwarf faction a natural historical storyline:

**What happened to the dwarves who lived here before us?**

Eventually they can discover that the ancient dwarves fought and died during the Six Day Battle.

---

## 10. Historical Knowledge in the Modern World

Different factions should possess different levels and versions of historical knowledge.

### Common humans

Know mostly legends and simplified history.

They may know that an ancient kingdom existed and that some catastrophic battle destroyed it.

Gudloth may be completely forgotten or reduced to an obscure legendary figure.

Dwarves were widely considered mythical or extinct until their recent return.

### Knights and scholars of La Poise

Possess conventional historical knowledge about the ancient kingdom and ruins.

They do not understand the true events surrounding Gudloth.

### Dwarves

Possess records from their own distant civilization.

These records may mention dwarves traveling east or establishing settlements centuries ago, but the fate of these dwarves is unknown.

### Elves

Preserve ancient events through oral history and cultural traditions.

Their version may contain genuine information unavailable to humans while also being distorted by centuries of retelling.

### Wizards

Possess the best surviving written records concerning the ancient world. Their predecessors were a smaller scholarly community in the Forge Kingdom, studying the world, nature, libraries, and poorly understood ancient magic before the battle forced them back toward the wizard regions.

Some wizard groups may know that Gudloth actually existed and that something significant happened to him during the Six Day Battle.

This information should be restricted rather than common knowledge.

Even the wizards should not necessarily know the complete truth.

### The Old City

The Old City is the northern remnant of the Forge Kingdom. It was abandoned for a long time after the Six Day Battle, then reinhabited by later generations as a new place built among the old ruins. It is not a continuously surviving Forge state or population.

Because its inhabitants live among the remains of the Forge Kingdom, the Old City may preserve structures, artifacts, records, and local knowledge unavailable anywhere else. Its current king is the legitimate descendant of the last Forge king and rules this later reinhabited city, not the Forge Kingdom at the height of its power.

The king is not the story's true villain. What the king and inhabitants know about the Six Day Battle and Gudloth remains to be determined.

For most of the world, the Old City and its king are simply unknown. People know that a king inhabits an old city built in the remains of the Forge Kingdom, but they do not possess reliable knowledge of the Six Day Battle, Gudloth, or the kingdom's true history. The early story should frame the king as mysterious and worth investigating, not as an openly evil ruler.

---

## 11. The Central Historical Mystery

The following broad sequence is currently established:

```text
The Visit
    ↓
Rise of the Forge Kingdom
    ↓
Rise of Gudloth
    ↓
Conflict between Gudloth and the Forge Kingdom
    ↓
Year 903: Six Day Battle
    ↓
Forge Kingdom destroyed
    ↓
Gudloth disappears
    ↓
817 years pass
    ↓
New civilizations and factions develop
    ↓
La Poise grows
    ↓
Dwarves return from another part of the world
    ↓
Modern faction conflicts
    ↓
The demon/player awakens
```

The major unresolved period is:

**What happened to Gudloth during the Six Day Battle that left him able to return only in a weakened form?**

This should remain unresolved until the nature of Gudloth's defeat is developed further.

Possible explanations have not yet been made canon.

# Modern Story

## 12. The Player Awakens

The game begins in Year 1720 After the Visit, on the ancient battlefield where the Six Day Battle occurred 817 years earlier.

The player controls Gudloth, returned as a weak demon/spirit-like entity.

Nobody in the modern world is waiting for this creature to return.

The player should not initially know they are Gudloth or be told they are the ancient villain. The weakened demon can appear to be a lost savior, hero, king, or another mysterious being with a justified grievance.

The battlefield knight does not recognize the player as Gudloth or as some legendary enemy. To the knight, the player is simply an unknown and dangerous creature emerging from an ancient ruin.

This separation between ancient history and the modern world is important.

The world has continued without Gudloth.

---

## 13. Prologue

The current opening sequence is:

1. The demon awakens extremely weak on the ancient battlefield.
2. It drains life force from a chicken to regain some strength.
3. It must get past the knight guarding the battlefield entrance.
4. It reaches the merchant road outside the battlefield.
5. It encounters potential hosts from the four major factions.
6. The player chooses their first major possession:
   - Dwarf
   - Elf
   - Wizard
   - Knight

This is the player's first major choice.

---

# Faction Story Structure

## 14. One Story, Four Perspectives

Choosing a faction should **not create four completely separate campaigns**.

Instead, the game has one progressing world story.

The player's first possession determines which perspective they initially experience.

The factions naturally encounter one another during the story, creating opportunities for the player to possess members of the other factions.

For example:

```text
Dwarves ← trade → Knights
                   ↕
             territorial war
                   ↕
                 Elves
                   ↕
              infiltration
                   ↕
                Wizards

Dwarves ← mountain exploration → Wizards
```

The exact connections can change as the quests are developed.

---

## 15. All Four Faction Arcs Are Required

Eventually, the player should experience the important storylines associated with all four factions.

The faction stories provide:

- Different perspectives on the modern world.
- Different perspectives on ancient history.
- Opportunities to unlock new possession types.
- Core gameplay abilities.
- Knowledge and access required for the main story.

The player may begin as any faction, but the different routes gradually converge.

The general story continues regardless of which faction the player currently possesses.

---

## 16. Race and Faction Abilities

Each possessed race should eventually provide abilities that function both mechanically and narratively.

Current conceptual examples:

### Dwarf

Associated with:

- Mining
- Breaking obstacles
- Ancient underground routes
- Dwarven ruins

### Knight

Associated with:

- Heavy combat
- Defense
- Authority
- Access to human-controlled areas

### Elf

Associated with:

- Mobility
- Stealth
- Forest traversal
- Hidden natural routes

### Wizard

Associated with:

- Magic
- Magical barriers
- Ancient seals
- Understanding supernatural phenomena

These abilities can eventually combine to make areas accessible that cannot be reached through a single faction alone.

---

## 17. Faction Conflicts Should Be Morally Ambiguous

The modern factions should not simply represent good and evil.

The player should encounter the same conflicts from different perspectives.

For example, La Poise may clear forest because its population needs food.

From the knight perspective:

**Protect farmers and settlers from elf attacks.**

From the elf perspective:

**Stop La Poise from destroying ancient forest.**

The underlying event is the same.

Possession changes how the player experiences it.

This structure should be used throughout the faction storylines where possible.

---

# Narrative Principles

## 18. Possession as a Story Mechanic

Possession should not function only as a class-selection system.

The body being possessed belonged to an actual person.

That person may have:

- Family
- Friends
- Enemies
- Responsibilities
- Debts
- Social status
- Existing relationships

NPCs therefore interact with the player according to the identity of the body they are currently using.

The demon may know very little about the person whose life it has stolen.

This can create both practical problems and moral consequences.

---

## 19. The World Does Not Revolve Around the Player

A central principle of the story should be that modern civilization developed independently after Gudloth disappeared.

The knights are not Gudloth's servants.

The dwarves did not return because of the demon.

The elf-human conflict is unrelated to the demon.

Most people have no idea that anything important has awakened.

The player enters a world that already has:

- Political conflicts
- Trade
- Families
- Territorial disputes
- Historical mysteries
- Cultural tensions

The demon gradually inserts itself into these existing systems through possession.

---

## 20. History Should Be Discovered Through Contradiction

There should not initially be one NPC who explains the true history of the world.

Each faction remembers history differently.

For example:

**Human interpretation:**

> The Forge Kingdom was an ancient civilization destroyed in a legendary war.

**Dwarven interpretation:**

> Our ancestors crossed the sea and established settlements here, but nobody knows what happened to them.

**Elven interpretation:**

> The old civilizations destroyed themselves and scarred the land.

**Wizard interpretation:**

> The stories contain fragments of something much more dangerous that has deliberately been forgotten.

The player gradually assembles these perspectives.

None needs to be completely correct.

---

## 21. Long-Term Main Story

The current intended broad progression is:

```text
Awaken on battlefield
        ↓
Drain life from chicken
        ↓
Escape battlefield
        ↓
First possession
        ↓
Enter faction storyline
        ↓
Faction conflicts begin intersecting
        ↓
Possess other races
        ↓
Unlock race abilities
        ↓
Discover contradictory pieces of ancient history
        ↓
Find growing evidence that Gudloth has returned and must be defeated
        ↓
Seek the mysterious king of the Old City for answers
        ↓
Gain access to the Old City
        ↓
Reveal that the player is Gudloth and the king is the rightful Forge heir
        ↓
Confront the king / discover the truth
```

The king is the rightful descendant of the Forge crown, but the nature of Gudloth's defeat, the king's knowledge, and Gudloth's ultimate objective remain open.

---

# Major Open Questions

The following should remain unresolved for now:

1. **What exactly was The Visit?**
2. **Why did Gudloth fight the Forge Kingdom?**
3. **What actually happened during the Six Day Battle?**
4. **What form did Gudloth's defeat, imprisonment, or sealing take before his return?**
5. **What happened during the centuries immediately following the Six Day Battle?**
6. **What does the current king know about the ancient events?**
7. **Why do the wizards protect the Old City and/or king?**
8. **Why does Gudloth ultimately need or want to reach the king?**
9. **How much of the true history has deliberately been hidden versus naturally forgotten?**

These questions form the foundation for the main-story mystery and should not be resolved solely to fill gaps in the timeline.
