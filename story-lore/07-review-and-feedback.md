# Review and Feedback

This feedback is assessed against the current combined canon in [Revised World History and Story Structure](Revised%20World%20History%20and%20Story%20Structure.md). It is design guidance, not a replacement source of canon.

## Overall Direction

The combined direction is considerably stronger than treating the old and new lore as competing versions. The Forge Kingdom, Gudloth, and Six Day Battle now give the world a genuine deep past, while La Poise, the elves, the newly arrived dwarves, and the wizards create immediate problems that exist without the player's involvement.

The strongest story promise is a weak body-stealing entity entering a world that has continued for centuries without it. The player is dangerous, but not initially important to anyone else. That contrast lets the game reveal ancient stakes gradually instead of opening with prophecy or exposition.

## What Works Best

### The Starting Image

An ancient battlefield is an excellent opening location because it communicates vulnerability and mystery before the player understands its history. The chicken drain gives the possession system a clear first use; the unknowing knight makes clear that no modern faction is waiting for Gudloth's return; and the merchant-road choice immediately places the player between the four major factions.

Keep the order explicit:

1. Wake weak on the Six Day Battlefield.
2. Drain the chicken.
3. Escape the guard and battlefield.
4. Choose the first lasting host: dwarf, elf, wizard, or knight.

The guard should be an obstacle, not an accidental fifth starting route.

### Modern Conflicts with Real Stakes

La Poise's expansion works because it answers a practical need. A city whose population needs food, roads, farms, and resources is not automatically villainous. The elves' resistance is equally justified when it protects forests, homes, and territory. Let each side do things that are understandable and things that are frightening.

The returned dwarves are especially useful because they provide a living historical investigation: they are newcomers in a place where their own people died centuries ago. Their discovery of ancient mines, graves, weapons, and partially readable inscriptions can make the history tactile rather than expository.

### Possession Creates Perspective

Possession should remain more intimate than a class-selection system. A body carries an identity, and the player benefits from a life that was not theirs. This naturally supports both faction access and moral tension:

- A knight's allies may rely on the player to protect settlers.
- An elf's community may trust the player with threatened land.
- A dwarf's expedition may expect help finding the fate of its predecessors.
- A wizard's peers may notice dangerous gaps in magical knowledge.

This lets each faction arc reveal not only different abilities but different costs of using power.

### Contradictory History

The revised approach to historical knowledge is right: no single NPC should explain the truth. Human scholarship, dwarf records, elf oral tradition, wizard archives, ruins, and the Old City should disagree in productive ways. The player assembles a pattern without receiving a simple answer too early.

## Areas to Protect

### Keep the Central Mystery Open

The player being Gudloth returned is established, but it should not be obvious to the modern world or to the player at the beginning. Let the weakened demon plausibly seem to be a savior, hero, king, or wronged being. The player should instead find mounting evidence that Gudloth has returned while investigating the distant, mysterious king of the Old City.

The reversal is that the player is Gudloth, the ancient villain, while the Old City's king is the legitimate descendant of the last Forge king and Gudloth's rightful opponent. Peak nature/life force and strong dark ancient magic enable Gudloth's weakened return in Year 1720; keep the circumstances of his survival, defeat, and sealing unresolved, and do not reduce the king to a simple final boss.

The player may eventually need to kill the king, but the current strength of the premise is that the game has not yet declared why. The king could be protector, prisoner, historian, obstacle, or something more complicated. Faction arcs should deepen that uncertainty before resolving it.

### Do Not Recreate the Forge Kingdom by Accident

The ancient Forge Kingdom is destroyed. Avoid presenting a modern hidden Forge state, a local dwarf population that survived continuously, or La Poise as Gudloth's empire unless a future canon decision intentionally changes this. Ancient ruins, legends, and traces are more compelling than a secret continuation of the old order.

### Keep Factions Distinct Without Simplifying Them

Each major faction already has a good specific hook:

- **Dwarves** are recent arrivals discovering a lost local past.
- **Knights** defend a hungry successor city making difficult expansion choices.
- **Elves** defend forests and territory while carrying imperfect old traditions.
- **Wizards** preserve rare records and understand that the supernatural past is dangerous.

Make those hooks visible in quests, architecture, dialogue, and routes. Avoid making any faction the universal source of truth or default villain.

## Practical Story Recommendations

- Let the player encounter the same human–elf conflict from both sides, with changed objectives but a shared underlying event.
- Use dwarf finds and wizard records to reveal different parts of the Six Day Battle; let elf traditions challenge both.
- Gate the Old City through a combination of faction abilities, access, and historical knowledge rather than raw combat power alone.
- Give possession social consequences that are legible, such as a friend noticing changed behavior or a duty the host is expected to fulfill.
- Make altars useful and unsettling, then decide later whether their origin is ancient Forge, wizardly, demonic, or something else.
- Treat active Gudloth loyalists, a surviving Forge Barrier, or a fixed royal-wizard alliance as future choices, not assumptions.

## Short Version

The story's core is now clear: a weak demon awakens after a forgotten catastrophe, steals lives to move through a morally divided modern world, and gradually learns that every faction holds a different fragment of the truth. Preserve the mystery, let the factions remain morally mixed, and use possession to make every new ability feel like entry into someone else's real life.
