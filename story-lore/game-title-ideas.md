# Game Title Shortlist

This shortlist retains only titles that serve all three core requirements:

- They belong to this world's ruined kingdoms, ancient god, rightful king, or Forge-era history.
- They connect to the player inhabiting, borrowing, or stealing other lives.
- They hint at the ending without revealing it outright.

## The Reveal to Protect

The player begins believing they are the weak returning spirit of the defeated Forge ruler. They appear to be growing stronger in order to take revenge on Gudloth and kill the ruler in the northeast old city.

The late-game reveal is that the player is actually **Gudloth**: the ancient demigod conqueror has returned as a weakened, fragmented, amnesiac spirit. The supposed villain in the old city is the rightful king. The player has unknowingly enabled the return of the ancient enemy they thought they were hunting.

## Current Top Titles

- Forger King
- Forger: The Hollow King

## Ranked Titles

1. **The Hollow King** — The player initially reads this as the dead Forge ruler's empty remnant; after the reveal, it describes Gudloth as a hollowed, incomplete god wearing a false claim to kingship. It implies possession through a spirit that needs other bodies, but is close in sound and structure to *Hollow Knight*.
2. **The Stolen Crown** — The crown belongs to the rightful king, while the player steals bodies, identities, power, and eventually a claim to rule. It supports the reveal that the player never had the crown they believed they were reclaiming.
3. **The Borrowed King** — Directly expresses the possession mechanic: the player lives through borrowed bodies. It also foreshadows that their royal identity and revenge story are borrowed or false.
4. **God of Many Faces** — Frames each possessed host as another face worn by the player, while quietly indicating that the spirit is a returning god rather than a lost king.
5. **The King Without a Name** — Works for an amnesiac player inhabiting others, and gains a second meaning when the forgotten name is revealed to be Gudloth's.
6. **The Unremembered King** — Suggests a forgotten ruler returning through scattered lives, while leaving open whether that ruler deserves the title “king.”
7. **The Nameless God** — Points toward Gudloth's buried divine identity and the world's fear of the last enemy; the many hosts can be understood as a god searching for a self.
8. **The Forgotten Sovereign** — Connects a lost identity, contested rulership, and the ancient political history the player mistakenly believes is theirs.
9. **The Broken Name** — The player's name, history, and sense of self are broken apart across possession and lost memory; the true name changes the meaning of the entire journey.
10. **A God Unmade** — Describes Gudloth's diminished state and his reconstruction through stolen bodies, abilities, and memories.
11. **The Last Enemy** — An in-world name for the being the wizards hunt. It hints that the player is the old world's surviving threat while their use of hosts keeps that threat hidden.
12. **The Forgotten Name** — The most understated identity-focused option: every host hides the player until the ancient name returns.
13. **The Crown of Ash** — The ash recalls the Forge Kingdom's defeat and battlefield, while the crown points toward the player's mistaken inheritance and the rightful king they pursue.
14. **The Revenant Crown** — A dead power returns through a spirit, but the crown remains deliberately ambiguous: it may be a false inheritance, a stolen claim, or Gudloth's old authority.
15. **Crown of the Revenant** — Similar to *The Revenant Crown*, with more emphasis on the returning entity rather than the political object it seeks.
16. **The Forged Revenant** — Ties the player to the Forge Kingdom's legacy and a return from death, while leaving room for the reveal that the assumed Forge connection is misleading.
17. **Ruinbearer** — The player carries an ancient ruin into every life they inhabit: the ruined Forge legacy on the surface and Gudloth's returned catastrophe underneath.
18. **The Flame Remains** — A poetic title for an old power surviving as a weak spirit, reappearing through borrowed lives in the aftermath of the Forge-era war.

## Recommendation

**The Stolen Crown** is the strongest alternative to **The Hollow King**. It is short, directly tied to possession and the rightful king, and its meaning changes cleanly after the reveal. It also avoids the immediate association with *Hollow Knight*.

**The Hollow King** remains the strongest title for the emotional and mythic shape of the twist, but should be tested carefully because of that association.
