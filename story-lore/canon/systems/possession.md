# Drain and Possession

**Status: Canon mechanic baseline.** Exact target thresholds, costs, and persistence rules remain open design decisions.

The returned demon survives and progresses by draining living targets and possessing weakened living hosts. Possession is not merely a class switch: every host is an existing person whose relationships, rank, obligations, inventory, and enemies continue to affect how the world responds.

## Current Interaction

1. Hold the TAKE OVER input (`Q`) on an eligible target to drain it.
2. Draining weakens the victim while exposing the demon to interruption.
3. Release the input before the victim dies when the target is weak enough to become a host.
4. Tap `Q` on that weakened, living target to possess it.
5. Holding the drain until completion kills the target and restores the demon instead of creating a host.

The opening uses this rule: the demon drains a chicken on the battlefield, escapes the guard, and reaches the merchant road. The guard is not automatically possessed or the first lasting host. The first major host is a dwarf, elf, wizard, or knight.

## Possession Results

- The host grants faction identity, abilities, and a temporary social position.
- The demon retains its core identity and can retain a persistent inventory and part of its progression between hosts.
- Hosts can have body-specific inventories, attributes, strengths, weaknesses, and level limits.
- NPCs can react to changed behaviour, missed duties, relationships, and faction identity.
- Switching, interruption, detection, and return-to-demon rules need clear player feedback.

## Still to Decide

- What interruption costs and how a player safely returns to demon form.
- What persists between hosts and whether former hosts can be revisited.
- How faction targeting, friendly AI, active quests, inventories, memories, and disguises update when possession succeeds.
- Whether altars are required switching points, optional stabilisation points, or serve another role.
