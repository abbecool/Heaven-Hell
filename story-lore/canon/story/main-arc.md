# Main Story Arc

**Status: Canon story structure.** The exact content and order of faction quests remain a design direction.

## Prologue

1. In Year 1720, a weak demon or spirit awakens on the Six Day Battlefield.
2. It drains a chicken to recover enough strength to continue.
3. It escapes the knight guarding the battlefield exit. The knight sees an unknown dangerous creature, not a returning Gudloth.
4. It reaches the merchant road.
5. It encounters a dwarf, elf, wizard, and knight as potential hosts.
6. The player chooses the first major possession.

The guard is an obstacle, not the first lasting host. See [Chapter One](chapters/01-awakening.md) and [Possession](../systems/possession.md).

## One World, Four Perspectives

The initial host determines the first perspective, not a separate campaign. Dwarves, knights, elves, and wizards participate in one advancing world story; later contact creates opportunities to inhabit the other groups. The player ultimately experiences all four important arcs.

| Host | Narrative and traversal access |
|---|---|
| Dwarf | Mines, obstacles, underground routes, metalwork, and the investigation of lost local dwarves. |
| Knight | Heavy combat, defence, authority, and human-controlled spaces. |
| Elf | Mobility, stealth, forest travel, and hidden natural routes. |
| Wizard | Magic, barriers, seals, archives, and supernatural understanding. |

Suggested intersections include dwarf trade with La Poise, the human–elf territorial conflict, elf infiltration of wizard spaces, and dwarf exploration toward wizard territory. The routes should converge around shared conflicts, abilities, and discoveries.

## Historical Reveal Structure

Each faction arc reveals both a present-day conflict and an incomplete account of the Forge Kingdom and Six Day Battle. Human records, dwarf discoveries, elf traditions, wizard archives, ruins, and the Old City should conflict productively rather than offer a single exposition source.

Early and middle-game evidence establishes that Gudloth has returned and must be defeated. The Old City and its king remain distant mysteries. The player can plausibly believe they are a savior, hero, king, or another wronged being. The late revelation overturns this framing: the player is Gudloth, while the king is the legitimate descendant of the Forge crown and Gudloth's opposing heir.

## Long-Term Progression

```text
Awaken on battlefield
        ↓
Drain life and escape
        ↓
First possession
        ↓
Enter a faction perspective
        ↓
Possess other factions and unlock abilities
        ↓
Navigate intersecting modern conflicts
        ↓
Discover contradictory ancient evidence
        ↓
Find evidence that Gudloth has returned
        ↓
Investigate the king of the Old City
        ↓
Gain access to the Old City
        ↓
Learn the player is Gudloth and the king is the rightful Forge heir
        ↓
Confront the king and discover the truth
```

The king's role, the player's exact reason for reaching the city, and the outcome of their confrontation remain open.
